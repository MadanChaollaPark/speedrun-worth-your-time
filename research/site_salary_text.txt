01 · The role
What Is a Founding Engineer?
A founding engineer is one of the first engineers at a startup, usually somewhere in the first five hires, brought on before the product has proven itself. The work is not a slice of a system; it is the system. You ship version one of everything, own whatever breaks, make the architecture calls that stick for years, and interview the engineers who come after you.
It is not a founder role. Founders hold founder stock and take the cap-table risk; a founding engineer is the earliest kind of employee, with a real salary plus an options grant that is large by employee standards and small by founder standards. The trade is deliberate: less cash than a big company would pay, more ownership and more surface area than a big company could ever offer.
The "founding" prefix has also spread past engineering. On this board right now there are 82 founding-titled roles: 38 in engineering, plus founding account executives, founding designers, founding recruiters, and first hires of most other crafts. 03 · Equity
Founding Engineer Equity, and What It Might Be Worth
Salary is the posted half of founding compensation. The other half almost never appears in a posting: of the 82 founding roles open right now, essentially none publish an equity number. Market practice is roughly
0.5% to 3%
, highest for the very first engineer at a pre-seed company, stepping down with each hire and each priced round. Grants are almost always stock options on a four-year vest with a one-year cliff.
Two things eat a grant between offer and exit: dilution, because every priced round issues new shares (commonly 15% to 25% per round), and the strike price you pay to exercise. 05 · FAQ
Founding Engineer Questions, Answered With Data
What Is a Founding Engineer?
A founding engineer is one of the first engineers at a startup, usually employee one through five, hired before the product has found its market. The job is to build version one across the whole stack, set the technical culture every later hire inherits, and trade some cash compensation for a meaningful equity stake. It is an employee role, not a co-founder role: you get a salary and options rather than founder stock.
How Much Does a Founding Engineer Make?
Across the 35 open founding roles with posted pay on the a16z speedrun talent network right now, the median posted band runs $160k to $227k, and the median band midpoint is $200k. The middle half of postings sits between $175k and $219k at the midpoint. These are posted figures from live job postings, recomputed continuously, not survey estimates.
How Much Equity Does a Founding Engineer Get?
Typical founding engineer equity runs roughly 0.5% to 3%, highest for the first engineer at a pre-seed company and stepping down with each hire and each funding round. Almost no posting publishes an exact equity number, so treat the range as market practice rather than a posted figure, and use the calculator on this page to reason about what a grant could be worth after dilution.
Is a Founding Engineer a Founder?
No. Founders hold founder stock, sit on the cap table from day zero, and usually take below-market salaries. A founding engineer is the earliest flavor of employee: real salary, an options grant measured in single-digit percentages or less, and typically a four-year vest with a one-year cliff. The title signals scope and timing, not ownership class.
Where Are Founding Engineer Jobs Located?
Of the 82 founding roles open across the a16z portfolio right now, the top cities are San Francisco (39), New York (19), KSA - RYD G (3), and 9% are open to remote. Founding roles concentrate where the earliest-stage companies are, which in this corpus means San Francisco and New York first.
